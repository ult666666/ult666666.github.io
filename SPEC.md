## Structure and interaction

- Open with Charles’s name, two sentences about his studies and his work, an Email button, a GitHub button and his city, beside the real Snowfall OS village.
- Show the five featured projects as screenshots on coloured mats, each with one or two sentences, its main tools and its links. The financing model leads at full width.
- List all 21 public projects in the homepage index, one line each: what it is, the tools used and a relevant next step. Include the Snowfall OS sample village and the JUST-QR generator. Describe Teen Budgeting as a class project. Say that the trading research project can’t place orders.
- Make the explorer searchable by words and focus area. Keep project names, outcomes, tools and primary links easy to scan. Put longer technical walkthroughs behind a keyboard-operable disclosure. Project entries and links should work with JavaScript turned off.
- Use About to show how Charles checks the results of his work, with links to two write-ups. Put the contact sentence and the published studio email on a dark card at the end.
- Use a near-white page, dark ink and one system sans-serif. Colour comes from the screenshots and their mats. On phones the gallery becomes a single column and each index row becomes a short entry. Keep the technical attribution and prompt history in the repository.

## Delivery

Publish the reviewed files to the existing GitHub Pages site. Include this specification and the prompt log in the source repository. Verify that the deployed homepage, the public repository and project navigation work for a signed-out visitor.
