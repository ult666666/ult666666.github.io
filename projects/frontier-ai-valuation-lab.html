Frontier AI Valuation Lab

A valuation and financing model for frontier AI labs. Set a capital goal, compare financing plans across scenarios, and keep a record of the decision and the math behind it.

The decision

A valuation is useful when it helps someone decide. Capital Strategy Studio asks which financing plans meet a capital goal and an ownership floor, how they compare across named scenarios, and what would change the choice.

The worked example tests three hypothetical rounds against two goals: raise at least $25bn, and let existing holders keep at least 88% of their stake. Plans A and B meet both; at a 90% floor only A does.

The result is a decision record: the goals, the alternatives, the assumptions, the comparison rule, the analyst’s choice and reasoning, and what would trigger a review. The ranking the tool computes stays separate from the person’s judgment.

Capital and ownership

Capital and ownership are separate goals. A plan qualifies only if it raises enough and leaves existing holders enough of their stake; keeping 90% of a 10% stake means holding at least 9% of the company.

The capital goal is an input. It is never inferred from the model’s cash-flow deficits, which include modeling conventions such as the treatment of stock compensation and cannot say how much cash a company needs.

The ownership limit tool on the Model page works out the largest round a given valuation allows before holders fall below the floor.

Scenarios

Each operating scenario sets all thirteen valuation assumptions, and every scenario is applied to every plan. The tool never drops an inconvenient case.

For a modeled pre-money equity value M, a raise R and a retained fraction r, the comparison metric is r × (M + R): the modeled value that ends up with existing holders. It can be negative, and it is not an investment return.

The tool reports each plan’s regret against the best eligible plan in each scenario, and the break-even equity values where rankings flip. For Plans A and B the lines cross at M = $635bn. Below that value B leaves existing holders more; above it, A does.

Monte Carlo

Three cases hide the spread between and around them. A seeded Monte Carlo panel puts ranges on up to five drivers, such as revenue growth, terminal margin and the discount rate, joins them with a Gaussian copula, and reruns the same DCF engine for every draw.

With the default ranges around the Anthropic Base case, the median value is $364.2B, and nine draws in ten fall between $197.2B and $694.6B. These figures describe the ranges entered, not a forecast.

The browser and the API return byte-identical JSON for the same request, and an independent Python script checks the random numbers, the copula, the percentiles and the tornado chart.

How it is built

The financial math lives in plain JavaScript modules. The browser and a stateless Node.js API run the same modules on the same validated input, and the browser compares their complete results.

Editing any financial input invalidates the last API result, and a revision token stops a late response from confirming an edited decision. Exports and share links keep the inputs as entered; an import is recalculated, not trusted.

Separate Python scripts redo the arithmetic, the capital one with Decimal cash flows and Fraction shares, so a formula bug shows up as a disagreement. Running it needs no API key, database or paid data feed.

Try it

The objective preview tests new goals against your current plans before you apply them. In the worked three-plan example:

  1. Open the worked decision. With a $25bn goal and an 88% floor, Plans A and B are eligible.
  2. Preview objective changes and try a 90% floor: only A remains. Previewing does not change your draft.
  3. Preview $30bn with 90%: no plan qualifies. Return the floor to 88% and B qualifies.
  4. Choose Apply to change only the capital objective and retention floor. Plan terms and decision notes stay intact.
  5. To compare A and B, explicitly apply $25bn and 88% again, then read the value each leaves existing shareholders and their break-even equity value.
  6. Record a preference, its trade-off, a review trigger and what you still need to know, then export the decision or read the worked brief.

Limits

  • The company figures rest on illustrative assumptions and incomplete public disclosures. They are not valuations of Anthropic or OpenAI or investment advice, and the project is not affiliated with either company.
  • Scenario counts are not probabilities, and three presets cannot show that a choice is robust.
  • The round model keeps the proceeds as cash and leaves out fees, preferences, option pools, conversions, secondary sales and later rounds. It does not estimate how much a company needs to raise.
  • Monte Carlo draws are equally weighted, the copula has no tail dependence, and the tornado chart varies one driver at a time.
