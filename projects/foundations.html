01 Quantitative finance

quantkit

Option pricing with Black–Scholes–Merton formulas, Cox–Ross–Rubinstein trees and Monte Carlo; mean–variance and risk-parity portfolios; a vectorized daily backtester; and Value at Risk (VaR) model validation, in Python.

A strategy decides with prices through day t, and those weights earn day t+1's return. A checker changes later prices and fails any strategy whose earlier weights move.

Complete workflow: generate fictional prices, estimate monthly allocations from prior closes and compare on shared dates and costs. Inspect six CSV exports in the walk-forward guide and its timing regression tests.

VaR model validation: forecast one-day 99% VaR and expected shortfall (the mean loss beyond VaR) from the previous 250 days by historical simulation, a Gaussian model, EWMA and filtered historical simulation. Backtest the forecasts with Kupiec's and Christoffersen's tests, the Basel traffic light and the Acerbi–Székely Z2 test. The design notes show why the tests' own error rates matter: at 250 days a correct model fails the nominal 5% Kupiec test 10.5% of the time in simulation (9.5% exactly), and on fat-tailed Student-t(4) returns over 1,000 days, Z2 rejects Gaussian VaR in 87.3% of samples against 46.9% for the exception count.

02 Experimentation statistics

powerlift

Experiment design and analysis in Python: sample sizes and power, frequentist and Bayesian comparisons, multi-arm (A/B/n) tests, CUPED variance reduction, sample-ratio-mismatch checks and multiple-testing corrections.

Group sequential boundaries come from the joint normal distribution of the interim z-statistics, so a team can look early without inflating its false-positive rate.

Complete workflow: read a seeded per-user CSV, check allocation and the declared outcome window, then inspect a CUPED readout with uncertainty. The experiment walkthrough includes blocked scenarios and an inconclusive synthetic result.

PythonSciPyNumPySimulation
Run powerlift Methodology Design

The README's peeking simulation runs 10,000 A/A experiments (seed 2026): stopping at the first p < 0.05 across ten looks flags 19.5% of them, against a 5% target.

A/B/n tests: Dunnett's test compares every variant with one control and holds the family-wise false-win rate at 5%, with simultaneous intervals. In 2,000 simulated experiments with no real effect, it found a false win in 5.1% with three variants and 5.4% with six, where unadjusted tests found one in 13.5% and 20.4%. Multi-arm sample sizes and a k-arm CSV readout use the same distribution; see the A/B/n walkthrough.

From the same checkout, run the SciPy comparison and the family-wise error simulation.

uv run pytest tests/test_many_to_one.py -k "scipy_dunnett or family_wise" -v

Read max_t_cdf in _dunnett.py. Every treatment-versus-control statistic shares the control mean, so their correlations have a product form. Given the control's noise and the pooled variance they are independent, which turns a k-dimensional integral into a two-dimensional one that the trapezoidal rule evaluates deterministically.

The comparisons are many-to-one and single-step, and they pool the variance of all arms. With very unequal variances, pairwise Welch tests with an adjustment fit better.

03 Fintech backend

ledgerline

A double-entry ledger API built with FastAPI and SQLAlchemy. Journal entries are integer minor units that must balance per currency, posted transactions are immutable, and corrections are reversals.

Each write is one database transaction that replays the stored response for a repeated Idempotency-Key and compare-and-swaps every account version it touches, so concurrent requests cannot overdraw an account.

Holds (two-phase transfers): authorize an amount now, then capture all or part of it, void it or let it expire. Accounts report a ledger balance, the amount held and the available balance, and holds and postings both draw on the available balance, so held funds cannot be spent twice under concurrent requests. The API walkthrough runs each step over HTTP, and the design notes explain the state machine.

Complete workflow: run an isolated wallet over real local HTTP. Verify retry identity, overdraft rejection, reversal and historical balances, then reconcile and clean up. The wallet demo guide explains the receipt and disposable storage.

04 Analytics engineering

saas-analytics-warehouse

Analytics engineering for a fictional SaaS company. A seeded generator writes eight raw tables, a dbt and DuckDB warehouse models MRR movements, cohort retention and unit economics, and a report rebuilds its charts.

Complete workflow: trace a selected month from the MRR bridge to fictional customer records, payment-failure losses and channel/reason totals. The read-only investigation walkthrough reconciles exact cents and customer counts before writing three CSVs and a Markdown report. Payment-failure churn remains a subset of total loss.

Every customer-month's MRR change gets exactly one label (new, expansion, contraction, churn or reactivation), so the monthly bridge has to reconcile to the cent.

Incremental loads and history: six event and daily staging models load incrementally. Each run reprocesses a 7-day lookback window and replaces rows by unique key, so a row up to a week late is absorbed and a newer invoice version replaces the old one. A dbt snapshot records each customer's plan, seats, price, MRR and status as type 2 history on business dates. A load simulation lands the synthetic rows day by day, with a seeded 3% of eligible rows arriving one to five days late, and compares 24 relations with a full refresh by row hashes. At this size incremental runs are not faster, because the sources are CSV files read in full; the design decisions explain the trade-offs.

05 Search and retrieval

searchlite

A zero-dependency TypeScript search engine: Porter stemming, a positional inverted index, BM25F ranking across weighted fields, a query language with phrases, prefixes and fuzzy terms, highlighting, and hybrid retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion.

explain() rebuilds a result's score clause by clause and field by field, and a test requires it to equal the score the search returned.

Prune without changing a result: postings are stored as delta and variable-byte coded blocks of 128 documents with skip entries and a score bound per block. Queries run document at a time with MaxScore and block-max bounds, and property tests require the same documents, order and scores as scoring every match. At 10,000 synthetic documents, median top-10 latency fell from 2.06 to 0.30 ms on copies of the glossary and from 8.48 to 0.57 ms on Zipf-distributed labels, with byte-identical rankings (method and machine). Gains are smaller at 1,000 documents, one phrase-heavy control query is slower, and block-max WAND is not implemented.

Complete workflow: index a local documentation folder, save a snapshot and query it with snippets and score explanations. The local docs walkthrough uses the built library; searches read the snapshot and do not reread source files.

Review a retrieval change: open the relevance workbench, compare BM25F with stemming disabled, and inspect a query to see its ranked documents, judged grades and missed relevant entries. Download the full inputs and report. It matches the native command’s report: rankings and text exactly, scores to about twelve decimal places, because browsers’ math libraries can differ in the last digit. Aggregate gains can hide an individual query regression.

For your own notes, label questions and set per-query thresholds. A saved snapshot is enough to rerun the checks; unmet criteria return a distinct process exit code. The separate reproducible timing method records raw searches at 1,000 and 10,000 synthetic documents. Repeated glossary vocabulary and one machine's measurements do not establish production latency or general retrieval quality.

Use your own files: the local-file workbench compares two saved indexes against one judgments file in a browser worker. Inspect each query's rankings and download a report that matches the native command’s, with scores agreeing to about twelve decimal places. A synthetic fixture in the real browser tests has a positive average gain yet still fails an individual regression. Files stay in page memory; Clear releases application references. Reports contain supplied query text and document IDs, and the tool does not authenticate postings or labels. The workflow and limits explain bounded input handling and cancellation; real browser checks exercise report parity, offline use after loading and long-query layouts.

Inspect the execution: candidate-aware scoring narrows conjunctions and avoids scoring exclusions while preserving exact rankings and explanations. Paired worker-process measurements retain every raw trial, including slower cases and a broad-query control; their engine hashes identify the measured revision.

Challenge the vector arithmetic: aligned extreme vectors once returned NaN or zero and could lose top1 to an orthogonal document. The numerical boundary checks compare 96 synthetic cases with an independent high-precision reference, test mutation and snapshot round trips, and refuse sparse arrays atomically. Near cancellation and rounding still limit precision; the older timing report is not a measurement of this later numerical revision.

06 Accessibility and HCI

contrastkit

WCAG 2.2 contrast checks, perceptual OKLCH fixes for failing pairs, color-vision-deficiency simulation and palette audits: a zero-dependency TypeScript library, a CLI that can gate design tokens in CI, and a React app.

Milder deficiencies: besides the three dichromacies (a missing cone type), the simulation covers the more common anomalous trichromacies, protanomaly, deuteranomaly and tritanomaly, at any severity from 0 to 1. It uses all 33 matrices Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) published, interpolated linearly between their 0.1 steps, and severity 1 gives exactly the dichromacy. Audits and the CLI's repeatable --cvd option take a severity, and the Palette Auditor adds a keyboard-operable severity slider that shared links keep.

A fix keeps the hue and changes only OKLCH lightness, searching both directions for the closest passing color. Ratios are truncated, so a near miss never displays as a pass.

Complete workflow: download the current palette as tokens JSON, audit it with the CLI, apply the suggested fix and audit the next download. The web-to-CLI walkthrough preserves the selected pair and target; simulations do not change exported colors.

07 AI engineering

citecheck

Question answering over a document collection with the Claude API. Passages come from a SQLite FTS5 index ranked with BM25, the model must cite a retrieved passage for every sentence through a strict tool schema, and the app abstains without a model call when nothing relevant is found.

A checker rejects citations to passages that were never retrieved, splits each sentence into claims and compares every claim with the cited clause it restates. A flipped negation, "must" where the source says "may", a word swapped for its opposite, or a number or unit that differs makes the claim unsupported.

Measured on held-out cases: 140 hand-labelled claims were split by a hash of their IDs into 63 for tuning and 77 held out. On the held-out cases the claim-level checker catches 44 of 48 unsupported sentences where the earlier word-overlap checker catches 20, with 2 false flags on 29 supported sentences against 1 (exact McNemar p = 1.5e-06). One author wrote both the cases and the rules, so these figures are optimistic; the design notes list every remaining miss.

A boundary worth checking: headings inside fenced code examples keep their enclosing section citation. Real index and retrieval regressions exercise shorter or invalid closing fences and the genuine section that follows.
